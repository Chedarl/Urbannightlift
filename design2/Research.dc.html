What was borrowed, and what was refused

Meituan and Ele.me run 90% of a market of a billion people. Most of what makes them work does not transfer.

They are built for a catalogue of thousands of restaurants, every dish photographed, coupons stacked three deep. We have four merchants and almost no photographs. Copying the surface would produce a page that looks busy and says nothing. So this took the mechanics and left the merchandising.

Taken
The pinned cart bar

Both apps keep the running total and the way forward welded to the bottom of every screen inside a merchant. You never lose the thread, never wonder what it costs, never hunt for the button. The single most valuable thing in the study.

Ours adds the delivery fee to that line — the number people actually argue about, said before the next screen rather than after it.

The left category rail

Meituan's signature: categories pinned down the left, dishes on the right. A forty-item menu becomes two taps instead of forty scrolls, and you always know where you are in it.

Counts beside each label, so an empty category is visible before you tap it.

Density, deliberately

Western delivery apps give a dish a whole card. These give it a row — thumbnail, name, one line, price, stepper — and fit three in the same space. Designers call it cluttered; users call it fast.

Held against our 13px floor: dense, never small.

The note field, promoted

On Meituan the checkout note is the most-used control in the flow — it is where "no chilli" and "call at the gate" live. Ours was a hidden expander; it is a first-class row now.

In a city of landmarks rather than addresses, this field is half the delivery.

Refused, and why
A photo on every dish

Their menus always carry one. We have almost none — merchants send a logo sometimes and a dish photo essentially never. Copying this gives a page of grey rectangles, which reads as broken rather than sparse. The generated artwork stays: stable per restaurant, never mistakable for real food.

Coupon confetti

Banners, stacked discounts, flashing badges. That machinery is worth building at ten thousand merchants. At four, it is decoration pretending to be a marketplace — and this product has already been burned once for showing things it did not have.

The minimum order

起送价 blocks checkout below a threshold. It makes sense where a courier carries six orders at once. We have had zero completed orders; refusing a small one is the last thing this business needs. The minimum lives in the fare instead, where it is a price rather than a door.

And one finding that settled an argument

Meituan raises its delivery fee after 10 p.m. The largest food delivery platform in the world charges a late-night premium as ordinary practice.

Our 23:00 band is not a trick invented to squeeze a night market — it is the market norm, arrived at independently, and it can be defended with that sentence at the door. The difference is that ours is a stated hour rather than a surge: a premium you can predict from the clock is forgiven; one that appears because demand spiked is resented.
