# Start & grow — a working plan for Urban Night Lift

This is the operator's playbook, not a pitch. It maps each growth move to a
feature that already exists in the app, so nothing here needs new code to begin.
Urban Night Lift serves two customers at once: **people with no time in the day**
to run errands, send or receive things, and **small and medium businesses** that
use us as a night channel to reach clients and sell more. The plan grows both.

---

## Phase 0 — Before the first night (this week)

**Prove one loop end to end.** Place a real order yourself, from the portal,
through payment, dispatch, delivery OTP, and the goodbye + rating screen. If any
step feels slow or unclear as the customer, fix that before spending a franc on
reach. The product is the marketing here — a night delivery that arrives calm and
tracked is what gets retold.

- Keep **test mode** on until the first real, paid night.
- Seed **3–5 real merchants** by hand in admin (a pharmacy, two restaurants, a
  grocery). Call each one, confirm they trade at night, upload their logo. A
  short, true catalogue beats a long, stale one.
- Set the four live services only (Medicine, Food, Grocery, Parcel). The paused
  three already capture **"notify me"** demand — that is your market research,
  free, before you staff them.

## Phase 1 — The first 100 customers (weeks 1–6)

The goal is not volume, it is **repeat**. A hundred people who order twice beats a
thousand who order once.

1. **Two riders, one zone, one promise.** Start in Biyem-Assi and the green
   zones. A tight radius means fast, reliable nights — the reputation you want to
   spread. Widen only when a zone is consistently under 30 minutes.
2. **Referrals do the acquisition** (already built). 500 XAF off a customer's
   first order; the referrer earns on the friend's early orders. Every happy
   customer is handed a code on the confirmation screen. This is the Chowdeck /
   Glovo playbook: growth comes from the people already ordering, not from ads.
3. **Ambassadors carry the streets** (task #42 — the brand-ambassador rebuild).
   Recruit 5–10 people who are out at night — students, bar staff, salon owners —
   give them a code and 10% of the company's share on their customers' first ten
   orders. That is real money to them and a fixed, predictable cost to us. It is
   the cheapest reach in this market.
4. **Answer every night personally.** Use the CRM inbox. A first-week complaint
   answered in fifteen minutes with a goodwill credit turns a refund into a
   regular. Watch the SLA timer; nobody waits.

**What to measure:** repeat rate (>30% by week 6), median delivery time, and
CSAT from the rating flow. Ignore vanity totals.

## Phase 2 — The SMB channel (weeks 6–16)

This is the second business, and it is where margin scales without more riders.

- **Merchant self-signup is live** (`/merchant/join`). Walk it yourself, then send
  the invite link over WhatsApp to every merchant you already deliver from. A
  business that fills its own page is trading today and wants the orders.
- **Sell the outcome, not the app:** "We bring your kitchen to customers who are
  home at 11 PM and won't come out." Night is your wedge — most of their daytime
  competition is closed.
- Use the CRM **Merchants** view as an account-management surface: who sells,
  which nights are quiet, who has gone quiet themselves (the freshness flag).
  Call the quiet ones. That relationship is the product for the SMB side.
- Start with **five anchor merchants** whose names pull orders. Their customers
  become your customers; your riders become their delivery team.

## Phase 3 — Grow the map, not the chaos (month 4+)

- Add a **new zone** only when the current ones are reliably fast and a rider is
  idle. Growth that outruns delivery time destroys the reputation that referrals
  depend on.
- Turn on a **paused service** when its "notify me" list is long enough to fill a
  rider's night — the demand data is already sitting in admin.
- Web push is free and already wired: **new-order alerts to dispatch, assignment
  alerts to riders, status alerts to customers.** Turn it on as volume rises so no
  night order is missed — a missed 1 AM order is lost revenue you can't recover.
- Keep the honest ceiling in mind: reliable live tracking needs the rider's phone
  awake. When the fleet or theft risk justifies it, move to bike-mounted GPS
  (`docs/TRACKING-OPTIONS.md`). Not before.

---

## The one rule that holds all of it

**Every night has to arrive well.** Referrals, ambassadors, and merchants are all
just amplifiers — they multiply whatever the delivery experience actually is. Spend
the first months making that experience calm, fast, and safe, and the growth loops
already in the app do the rest.
